# 3.4 Assumptions

Write a substantive Assumptions section. Assumptions and Scope Exclusions are the two mandatory
baseline sections that every SAP RFP response MUST carry, so keep it firm and complete, not a
placeholder.

Structure: a 2-3 sentence lead paragraph, then a MARKDOWN BULLETED LIST of 8-14 assumptions,
grouped by theme in this order:
1. **SCOPE & METHODOLOGY**: Fit-to-Standard adherence, Best Practice activation, RICEFW/WRICEF
   ceilings from the workbook, the greenfield/brownfield stance if named, waves as defined.
2. **CLIENT RESPONSIBILITIES**: dedicated SMEs / process owners for every module in scope, timely
   UAT sign-off, decision turnaround SLAs, access to legacy systems and documentation, cutover
   freeze windows.
3. **DATA & INFRASTRUCTURE**: legacy data quality/cleansing by the client, standard migration
   templates, environments provisioned before Explore, network/latency baselines, standard SAP
   licensing and named-user counts as procured by the client.
4. **INTEGRATION & THIRD-PARTY**: the named third-party systems expose documented APIs / standard
   extract tables; vendor engagement and credentials are the client's responsibility; the
   integration technology is SAP Integration Suite (CPI) unless stated otherwise.
5. **COMMERCIAL & GOVERNANCE**: working-days convention, holiday calendar, onsite/offsite mix,
   single-shift working, change-request governance, validity period.

Every bullet is one crisp sentence. Ground the bullets in the actual modules, countries and
third-party systems from the context; do NOT produce a generic template. NO person-days, dollar
figures or USD/day rates in any bullet.

The theme labels may be bold lead-ins on the bullets (`- **Scope & methodology:** …`) or
`###` sub-headings. Do not number the themes in the output.

## Grounding (where each name and value comes from)
- Reference the specific SAP modules, non-catalogue tools and third-party systems this engagement
  actually touches. A generic template of assumptions is exactly what makes this section read as
  boilerplate. The views are given to you as **grounding** (system names, module list), not as
  figures to reproduce.
  - Modules and business areas: `scope_by_lob`. Countries and engagement type: `profile`.
  - Third-party systems and their interfaces: `integrations`. SAP tools: `non_catalogue`.
  - RICEFW/WRICEF ceilings: the object counts in `tech_dev`. Write them as bare numbers only
    exactly as stated, e.g. "up to 40 WRICEF objects of the stated complexity mix".
  - Waves and their sequencing: `timeline`. Name waves exactly as the ledger does.
- Onsite/offsite mix: state it as an assumption only when `resource_location` is not withheld.
  Otherwise write "the delivery model set out in this proposal".
- Validity period, holiday calendar and working-days convention: say they are as stated in the
  commercial terms. Give no number unless the RFP or the ledger states it, quoted.
- If the RFP names an assumption it wants confirmed (`excerpts:3.4` or a mapped requirement),
  answer it explicitly.

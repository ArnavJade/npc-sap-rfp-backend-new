# Governance sections: 5.1-5.3 and 5.5-5.11

These sections used to read as generic, repeated boilerplate because they saw nothing but
module and country counts. Their client-specific material is:
- **the RFP's own words** on governance, PMO, tools, reporting, escalation, quality and change
  control. Load `excerpts:<id>` and the mapped requirements and lead with them. This is the single
  most important thing.
- **Programme scale**: the modules in scope, the countries (`profile`), and the waves and their
  sequencing (`timeline`). For a multi-country roll-out, reflect wave and phase planning per
  country where relevant: per-wave steering, country leads, cutover governance per wave.
With neither, write 2-3 honest sentences of standard practice and stop. Never pad.

Keep each section distinct, as its title implies. Repeating the same content in several of them
is the defect to avoid.
- 5.1 PM processes and practices: planning, status reporting cadence.
- 5.2 PM tools: only tools the RFP names or that `reference/yash-profile.md` states.
- 5.3 Communication: audiences, channels, cadence.
- 5.5 Risks, issues and variations: the risk and variation plan.
- 5.6 Issue resolution: the issue workflow and turnaround.
- 5.7 Governance and escalation: governance bodies and escalation levels. A qualitative pipe table
  (Level | Forum | Participants | Trigger) is fine. No people's names.
- 5.8 Quality management: the quality framework.
- 5.9 Project quality: deliverable reviews and sign-offs.
- 5.10 QA control: quality gates and audits.
- 5.11 Change management and control: the change-request process for scope, cost and time.

Twelve governance sections in a row are where repeated opening shapes show most. Vary every
opening. Give no figures: no effort, cost, FTE or durations. SLAs and cadences come only from
the RFP, quoted.
